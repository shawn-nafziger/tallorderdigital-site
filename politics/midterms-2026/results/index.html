How the count moved

Leads change on election night because states count different kinds of ballots at different times.

Some count mail ballots first; others count them last.

Split tickets

Some states vote for a senator and a governor on the same ballot.

When the two races land far apart, many voters chose one party for one office and the other party for the other.

How many people have voted

Each dot is 100,000 voters.

Filled dots are votes counted so far in 2026; hollow dots fill out each state's 2024 presidential turnout.

Midterms usually draw fewer voters than presidential years, so the hollow dots are a yardstick, not a finish line.

How this was put together

  • 2026 results: downloaded from each state's official election-night results files and saved every 10 minutes. These are unofficial counts; states finish counting and reach over the coming weeks.
  • No race calls: this page says who leads and how much of the vote is in. It doesn't declare winners.
  • Margin: the leading two candidates' shares of the vote between them. In Nebraska, Idaho and South Dakota, where no Democrat is on the Senate ballot, the main independent candidate stands in for the Democratic side, and the row says so.
  • States loaded: Arizona, California, Colorado, Florida, Georgia, Iowa, Indiana, Minnesota, North Carolina, Nebraska, Ohio, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Texas, Virginia. Other races show only their past results until their counts are added.
