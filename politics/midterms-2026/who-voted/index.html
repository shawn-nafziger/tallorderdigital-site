Who voted how

Democrats came out about 12 points ahead in this year's House vote, according to the main of voters. Most of that edge came from cities and suburbs, because that's where most voters live: about 2 in every 3.

  • Cities: about 7 in 10 voters chose the Democrat.
  • Suburbs: about 6 in 10 chose the Democrat. This is the biggest group of voters.
  • Rural areas: about 6 in 10 chose the Republican.
  • But only about 1 in 6 voters lives in a rural area, so those votes didn't make up the difference.

How to read this page

Two kinds of evidence, never mixed.

Survey How groups of people voted, from The ABC News Voter Poll conducted by SSRS: about 79,078 voters nationwide and 2,000 to 5,000 in each of 28 state Senate and governor races.

Counts How places voted: every House district, with AP's race calls and Census profiles of who lives there.

A rural district doesn't tell you how each rural person in it voted. So this page never mixes the two.

How this was put together

  • The survey: The Voter Poll conducted by SSRS is the one survey AP and all the major networks used in 2026. It interviewed about 75,000 voters before Election Day (invited by mail, phone and text, and from online panels) and about 25,000 more in person at polling places in 15 states, then weights the answers to match the actual vote counts. Its tables were read from ABC News's pages; they were last updated Nov 4, 2026, 12:04 p.m. ET.
  • Survey margins of error: about ±0.6 points nationwide for the full sample; wider for small groups, for state races (about ±2 to 3 points) and for questions asked of half the sample. A gap of a point or two inside one group can be noise.
  • Lead: Democratic % minus Republican % among all voters in the group. In the survey charts, "D+12" means 12 more of every 100 voters chose the Democrat.
  • No race calls: this page reports AP's calls with their sources and says who leads in unofficial counts. Counts are unofficial until each state's .
  • Places vs. people: district profiles describe everyone who lives there, including people too young to vote and people who didn't vote.
