How big is Alaska, really?

Alaska has 2.2 times as much land as Texas. It's bigger than Texas, California and Montana combined, and about a fifth the size of the lower 48. It just isn't as big as most maps make it look.

How many Texases fit in Alaska?

About 2.2, counting only. Count lakes, rivers and coastal water too and it's about 2.5, because Alaska has far more water than Texas. California fits about 3.7 times, and Montana, the fourth-biggest state, about 3.9 times.

Cut Alaska in half. Would each half still be bigger than Texas?

How big is Alaska compared to the rest of the U.S.?

Alaska has about 19% as much land as the lower 48 states put together, about a fifth. It holds 16% of all U.S. land, and more land than Texas, California and Montana combined.

How big is Alaska compared to Europe?

Alaska has more land than mainland France, Spain and Sweden combined, the three biggest countries in the European Union. All 27 EU countries together have about 2.7 times Alaska's land.

Why does Alaska look so big on maps?

Most web maps use the projection, which stretches places more the farther they are from the equator. It draws Alaska about 5.3 times its real size and Texas only about 1.4 times, so on a web map Alaska looks about 8.5 times as big as Texas. It's really 2.2 times.

How many countries are bigger than Alaska?

Only 18 of the 193 have more land than Alaska, and one of them is the United States, which includes it. Its closest match is Mongolia, with about 5% more land.

How to read these

"Land area" leaves out lakes, rivers and coastal water.

Every outline is drawn at true size. That's why some look different from the map you know.

"Lower 48" means the 48 states that touch each other, without Alaska and Hawaii.

Countries with far-off parts are measured as their main body ("mainland France").

Notes and sources
