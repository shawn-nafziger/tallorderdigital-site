The shortest day of the year isn’t your darkest evening

Monday, Dec. 21, is the shortest day of 2026: the winter solstice arrives at 3:50 p.m. Eastern. Across 51 US cities, daylight that day runs from 10h 50m in Honolulu down to 3h 41m in Fairbanks. But in most of the country, the earliest sunset of the year is already about two weeks behind you by then.

By Shawn Nafziger · Published Nov. 16, 2026 · Computed from NOAA’s solar equations

When is the shortest day of the year?

In 2026 the shortest day of the year is Monday, Dec. 21, in every US time zone. The winter solstice, the moment the sun is farthest south, comes at 3:50 p.m. Eastern (20:50 UTC), and every one of the 51 cities below gets less daylight that day than on any other. The farther north, the shorter it is: 10h 50m in Honolulu, 5h 27m in Anchorage and 3h 41m in Fairbanks.

What time is sunset on the shortest day of the year?

On Dec. 21, latitude sets how long the day is, and your time zone sets when it ends. Each bar below is one city’s day, from sunrise to sunset on the clock. The farther north, the shorter the bar. That part is geometry.

Where the bar sits is politics: time zones. Boston and Detroit get the same 9h 05m of light on the solstice, on the same clock, but the sun sets at 4:15 p.m. in Boston and 5:03 p.m. in Detroit, which sits near the western edge of Eastern time. South Bend (5:18 p.m.) and Indianapolis (5:23 p.m.) sit even farther west, so their sunsets come after 5:15.

When is the earliest sunset of the year?

Not on the shortest day. In New York, the earliest sunset of the year comes on Dec. 8 (4:28 p.m.), 13 days before the solstice. Sunrises, meanwhile, keep getting later until Jan. 4 (7:20 a.m.). For the typical city of the 51, the earliest sunset comes 14 days before Dec. 21; in Boston it’s Dec. 8 (4:11 p.m.).

Why is the earliest sunset before the solstice?

Because the sun keeps its own schedule. Clocks assume the sun peaks at the same moment every day, but in December “solar noon” drifts about half a minute later each day (23 to 30 seconds), because Earth is speeding up toward its closest pass by the sun in early January and because of the planet’s tilt. Near the solstice the day is barely shrinking, so that drift wins: sunsets start getting later before the days start getting longer, and sunrises keep sliding later after.

How far apart the two dates fall depends on latitude. The farther south you are, the less the day length changes near the solstice, so the drift matters more: Honolulu’s earliest sunset comes on Nov. 26 and its latest sunrise on Jan. 16. In Fairbanks, where the days shrink and grow fast, both land within a few days of the solstice (Dec. 18 and Dec. 24).

After Dec. 21, does the light come back in the morning or the evening?

In the evening, in every one of the 51 cities. By Jan. 31, New York gains 50 minutes of daylight: 40 minutes at the end of the day and only 10 minutes at the start. Farther south the split is even more lopsided: in Tampa, Miami and Honolulu, mornings on Jan. 31 are still darker than they were on the solstice.

What it means for New York: the earliest sunset is Dec. 8 and the latest sunrise is Jan. 4. In between, the evenings are already brightening while the mornings are still darkening. By the spring equinox on March 20, you’ll have 12h 08m of daylight, 2h 53m more than on the shortest day.

How this was measured
  • Times are computed, not looked up. Sunrise and sunset come from NOAA’s solar calculator equations (based on Jean Meeus’s Astronomical Algorithms), run for each city’s downtown coordinates for every day from September 2026 through March 2027.
  • Sunrise and sunset are when the top edge of the sun meets a flat horizon, allowing for normal atmospheric bending of light. Hills, tall buildings and elevation can move what you see by a few minutes.
  • Checked against a second calculation (the open-source Astral library): over September to March, the median difference is 14 seconds and the largest under a minute (Fairbanks at the solstice).
  • The solstice moment, 20:50 UTC on Dec. 21, 2026 (3:50 p.m. Eastern), is as published by Royal Museums Greenwich and others; our calculation lands within a minute of it.
  • Clock times include daylight saving time, which ends Nov. 1, 2026, and starts March 14, 2027. “Daylight gained” is measured in standard time so the clock change isn’t counted as light.
  • Earliest sunset is the earliest from Nov. 2 to Jan. 31; latest sunrise the latest from Dec. 1 to March 13. Both are the exact turning points; around each, the clock time often holds within a minute for a week or more.
  • Farther north than these cities, the sun doesn’t rise at all for a while: in Utqiaġvik, Alaska, it sets on Nov. 18 and doesn’t rise again until Jan. 23.
