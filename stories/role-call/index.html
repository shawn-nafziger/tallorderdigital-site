Role Call

One week in, a player's shooting is noise. His role isn't. Here's whose role has really changed this season, and whose only looks like it.

By Shawn Nafziger · Data through Oct 27, 2026

0.83How well his minutes per game after 4 games predict the rest of his season
0.21How well his after 4 games predicts the rest of his season

How to read these: each number is a correlation, a score from 0 to 1.0 for how well a player's first 4 games predict the rest of his season. 0 means they tell you nothing; 1.0 means they predict it perfectly. Around 0.7 and up, early numbers are a good guide.

Coaches decide roles. Luck decides makes.

How many minutes a player gets, how often the offense runs through him and where he shoots from are decisions, made by coaches and players before the ball goes up. Whether the shot goes in is partly luck. Among the 367 players who played at least 40 games last season, the decisions showed up within 4 games. The makes didn't.

Whose role changed

Each dot is one player. Across: how his minutes per game changed since last season. Up: how his changed, meaning how much of the offense runs through him.

The gray box shows what a normal first week looks like: about ±10 minutes and ±6 usage points of random . Being inside the box isn't good or bad. It means we can't yet tell his change from noise.

Orange dots are outside the box: a change bigger than a noisy week explains, so it reflects a real decision about that player's role. 47 of 249 players are there. Light orange dots are at the edge: at least 80% of the way out of the box, so probably real but not yet certain. Whether that's good news depends on the player. More minutes for a second-year player is usually good; a smaller share for a star on a new team is a question.

Three kinds of change

  • New team, smaller share. Jaylen Brown (BOS to PHI) is using 24.9% of plays, down from 35.1%, while playing more minutes (34.4 to 36.8). Ja Morant (MEM to POR): 30.5% to 20.7%. Jonathan Kuminga (GSW/ATL to MIN) is playing 29.8 minutes a game, up from 23.1, but his usage fell from 22.6% to 14.2%.
  • Bigger share of the offense. Naz Reid (MIN to CHA): 21.3% to 28.1%. Brandon Miller: 27.2% to 34.4%. Duncan Robinson (15.2% to 21.3%) is the player behind Detroit's new shot diet. Anthony Edwards, already a high-usage star, is up to 36.7%, right at the edge of the box.
  • More minutes. Khaman Maluach went from 8.9 to 23.4 minutes a game. Dylan Harper (22.6 to 31.1) is at the edge of the box: probably real, but not yet beyond what a noisy week could do.

Same team, smaller role happens too: Alex Sarr's usage is 13.8%, down from 25.2%.

Roles can still change with an injury, a trade or a lineup tweak. But a role change this size in week 1 is a decision, not a fluke.

Method

  • Data: player box scores for the 2025-26 regular season and 2026-27 through Oct 27 (56 games), from the NBA Stats API via the open-source nba_api client.
  • Usage % is the NBA's per-game usage, averaged with each game weighted by minutes played. True shooting % = points ÷ (2 × (FGA + 0.44 × FTA)), from totals.
  • Predictive test: among the 367 players with 40+ games in 2025-26, the correlation between each player's first 4 games and his games 5 to 82. Rate stats need 40+ minutes in the first 4 games. Part of the high correlation for minutes and usage comes from stars staying stars, which is why changes are judged against the wobble instead.
  • Wobble: the standard deviation of (first 4 games − full season) for the same players: 5.2 minutes per game and 3.0 usage points. The box is ±2 of those. "At the edge" (light orange) means at least 80% of the way to the box's edge. Players with 3 games played wobble a bit more.
  • Who is shown: 249 players with 3+ games this season and 20+ last season who play 15+ minutes a game in either season. 27 rotation players without a 2025-26 baseline (rookies and returning players) aren't shown.
  • Limits: one season of history for the test. The story describes what changed, not why; it doesn't account for injuries, opponents or blowouts.

Data: NBA Stats API via the open-source nba_api client. Not affiliated with or endorsed by the NBA or any team.
