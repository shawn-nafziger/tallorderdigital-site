True shooting percentage

Published Nov 14, 2026 · League numbers through Nov 13, 2026

True shooting percentage (TS%) measures how efficiently a player scores: points per shooting attempt, counting threes at their full value and counting free throws. The NBA average was 58.1% last season (2025-26) and is 57.3% so far this season, through Nov 13, 2026.

The true shooting formula

TS% = points ÷ (2 × (FGA + 0.44 × FTA))

FGA is field-goal attempts (every two and three) and FTA is free-throw attempts. The bottom of the formula counts a player's shooting attempts, and dividing by 2 puts the result on the same scale as field-goal percentage: a player who only ever shoots twos, and never gets to the line, has a TS% equal to his FG%.

Why 0.44? Free throws come in ones, twos and threes. Two free throws use one possession, a fouled three-point shot uses one for three free throws, and an and-one adds a free throw to a shot already counted. Across a season, each free-throw attempt works out to about 0.44 of a shooting attempt, so FGA + 0.44 × FTA is close to the number of times a player tried to score.

True shooting is always worked out from a player's totals for the season, not by averaging his game-by-game percentages.

What is a good true shooting percentage?

Among the 138 players who played 40+ games and took 10+ shooting attempts a game in 2025-26, the middle player was at 58.0%, the top quarter were at 60.8% or better and the top 10% at 62.7% or better. Below 56.5% was the bottom quarter. The league average, 58.1%, sits almost exactly in the middle.

A rule of thumb for what a point of true shooting is worth: for a player taking 20 shooting attempts a game, each extra percentage point of TS% is worth about 0.4 points a game.

True shooting vs field-goal percentage

In 2025-26, Stephen Curry made 46.8% of his shots and Nikola Jokić 56.9%, a gap of 10.1 percentage points. Count Curry's 190 threes and 221 free-throw attempts and the gap in true shooting shrinks to 3.3 (67.0% vs 63.7%). Field-goal percentage treats a three like a two and ignores free throws; true shooting counts both, which is why it's the better single number for scoring efficiency.

How long until a player's true shooting means something?

Months. A player's true shooting after his first 4 games barely predicts the rest of his season (a correlation of about 0.21 in 2025-26), because a few dozen shots carry a lot of chance wobble. That's why our dashboards and stories don't call an early-season jump in TS% real until it's well beyond the normal wobble.
