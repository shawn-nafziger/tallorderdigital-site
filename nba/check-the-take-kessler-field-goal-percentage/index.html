Kessler's 68%: finishing or the shots?

Walker Kessler is making 68.0% of his shots for the Los Angeles Lakers. Most of that comes from where he shoots: 84% of his attempts are at the rim, the most of any regular shooter in the NBA. His finishing on top of that is good, but not yet more than chance.

By Shawn Nafziger · Data through Nov 15, 2026 (14 games, 125 shots)

The verdict

The take

In ESPN's power rankings on Nov 11, Dave McMenamin named Kessler the Lakers' best newcomer and said Kessler has had "a major impact": 14.9 points, 10.8 rebounds and 69.1% from the field through 12 games (ESPN's rankings as republished by TSN).

A 69.1% field-goal percentage is eye-catching, but it mixes two things. One is where a player shoots: a dunk goes in far more often than a hook shot. The other is how well he converts from those spots. The first settles within a few games; the second takes months. So we split them.

How much of Kessler's 68% is shot location?

Most of it. We valued each of his 125 shots at what the whole league makes from that spot this season. That's his : what an average player would shoot on exactly his shots. It comes to 62.3%, against a league average of 53.5%. Where he shoots is worth +8.8 pp before he does anything special.

He then beats that by +5.7 pp, for 68.0%. (He hasn't taken a three, so his is just his field-goal percentage.) On 125 shots, though, that extra part would wobble about ±4.0 pp by chance alone, so a +5.7-pp edge is only 1.4 times the wobble. It could be skill; it could still be a good few weeks.

Where does Kessler shoot from?

Almost entirely at the rim. 105 of his 125 shots (84%) came within about four feet of the hoop, where the league makes 65.8%. A typical NBA player takes 27% of his shots there. The other 18 came in the rest of the paint, and he's taken just 2 from farther out.

At the rim he's made 74.3%, +8.5 pp better than the league. On 105 shots, that's 1.8 times the normal wobble: at the edge, close to the bar for beyond luck but not over it. His other paint shots (38.9% on 18, league 44.5%) are within chance.

Does anyone take easier shots than Kessler?

No one with 100 or more shots this season. His expected eFG% of 62.3% leads all 143 of them; next is Zion Williamson at 60.1%. Williamson is second in rim share too (70% of his shots, vs Kessler's 84%). Shot location is a choice, and it settles within a few games, so this part of his 68% will last as long as his role does.

Is his finishing better than chance?

Not yet. Among the 143 players, his +5.7 pp of finishing above expected ranks 26th. To clear twice the wobble on 125 shots, he'd need about +8.0 pp.

One game carries a lot of it. Kessler went 12-for-12 in his Lakers debut on Oct 21 against Golden State. Since then he's 73-of-113 (64.6%), just +2.5 pp above what an average player would make on those shots. That's a fine finisher getting great looks, not yet a great finisher.

Are the Lakers better with Kessler on the floor?

Yes, by a lot, though it's not yet beyond luck. The Lakers are +13.4 points per 100 possessions with him on the floor and −1.8 without him, a gap of 15.2. That's 1.9 times the normal wobble: at the edge. It ranks 37th of 332 qualified players.

Treat with care this early. It rests on 512 Lakers possessions without him, about five games' worth, and it measures lineups as much as one player: when Kessler sits, the Lakers have no backup big man, as McMenamin points out. 50 of 332 players clear the luck line, more than the 15 chance alone would give, a sign the simple luck math understates how much on/off swings.

McMenamin's take holds up: Kessler has been a big part of a 12–2 start, and his 68.0% is real. But it's mostly a product of his shots. He takes the easiest shots in the league, and an average player would make 62.3% of them. His finishing on top of that is promising, at the edge at the rim, but 14 games can't separate it from a hot start yet. Expect the location part to last, and his percentage to drift down toward the 62.3% his shots are worth.

Method

  • Data: NBA.com box scores and shot locations via the open-source nba_api client, 2026-27 through Nov 15, 2026. The numbers are frozen at that date from the site's Player Profile and Hot and Cold Shooters dashboards, which use the same data and math.
  • Expected eFG%: each shot valued at this season's league make rate for its zone (rim, rest of paint, midrange, corner 3, above-the-break 3), times 1.5 for threes. It knows location only, not defender distance, shot type or who passed the ball.
  • Finishing (shot making): actual eFG% minus expected eFG%. Its (4.0 pp) comes from the shot-by-shot spread. Beyond luck = more than 2 standard errors; at the edge = at least 1.6.
  • Rim FG%: tested against the league's rim rate with a standard error of √(p × (1 − p) ÷ shots) = 4.6 pp on 105 shots. Zones with fewer than 10 shots aren't judged.
  • Comparison pool: the 143 players with 100 or more shots this season.
  • On/off: on = 100 × his plus-minus ÷ his possessions; off = the Lakers' point differential without him, per 100 of their possessions without him. Luck band from a 14.4-point single-game spread in net rating. It doesn't adjust for teammates, opponents or garbage time.
  • Not checked: assisted vs unassisted shots, lobs and pick-and-roll play types (play-by-play and Synergy), and how closely each shot was contested.
