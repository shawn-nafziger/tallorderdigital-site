Hot streak or just luck?

Flip a fair coin 50 times and you'll rarely get exactly 25 heads. Flip 260 fair coins 50 times each and about 9 of them will come up 33 heads or more, or 17 or fewer. They look hot or cold, but every coin is fair.

Shooting works the same way. Even an exactly average shooter makes more shots some weeks and fewer others, and the fewer shots he takes, the bigger those swings get. That swing is his .

How to read this

  • Shooting % here is : field-goal percentage with threes counted 1.5 times, because they're worth 1.5 times as much.
  • An average shooter: what a league-average shooter would hit taking the same shots from the same spots this season. A player who takes mostly layups should shoot a higher % than one who takes mostly threes, so this is the fair comparison.
  • Himself last season: some players always shoot better than average, and some never do. This asks whether he's beating an average shooter by more than he did last season (he needs 100+ shots last season, so rookies aren't checked).

What it doesn't say

How closely each shot was defended, or whether a streak will last. A few games of shooting is the noisiest number in basketball, and most streaks fade.

"Last 5" means his last 5 games played, not his team's.

Method, for the stats-minded

Shooting % is effective field-goal percentage: eFG% = (FGM + 0.5 × 3PM) ÷ FGA, from every shot in the window. It's a total over his shots, never an average of game percentages.

An average shooter is expected eFG%: each shot is valued at this season's league-wide make rate for its zone (restricted area, paint, midrange, left and right corner 3, above-the-break 3, backcourt) × shot value ÷ 2. This season's numbers match Player Profile's "shot making". Shot making = actual − expected eFG%, the per-shot mean of (eFG value − expected value).

Luck range = ±2 standard errors of that mean. The standard error comes from the spread of his own per-shot gaps: SE = s ÷ √n. On a typical mix of shots s ≈ , so the range is about ± pp on 20 shots and ± pp on 100.

Verdicts use z = gap ÷ SE. Beyond luck: |z| > 2 (a two-sided test at about the 95% level, p ≈ 0.046). Maybe: 1.6 ≤ |z| ≤ 2. Could be luck: |z| < 1.6. This is the site's one bar for every color.

Himself last season: gap = (shot making now) − (shot making in 2025-26), each season measured against its own league's zone rates. SE = √(SEnow² + SElast²). It needs 100+ shots last season.

Many players, many chances. With N players checked, about 4.55% × N would clear |z| > 2 even if every one were exactly average (a multiple-comparisons problem). The page shows that count rather than adjusting the bar, so a reader can compare the two numbers. The headline calls the list "more than luck" only when hot + cold exceeds that count by more than 2 standard errors of a binomial(N, 4.55%).

Coin example: 50 flips of a fair coin, 33+ or 17- heads: exact binomial probability 3.3%, so about 9 of 260 coins.

Why this season's rates:

Limits: zones are coarse (a wide-open corner 3 and a tightly contested one count the same) and defender distance isn't used. The normal approximation is rough on 20–30 shots, and streaks regress: a player beyond luck over 5 games is still likely to fall back toward his usual level.
