Is Wemby living at the rim?

Victor Wembanyama is taking more of his shots at the rim: 40.7% this season, up from 35.7% last season. That's the right direction, but after 14 games it's still within normal chance wobble. What is clear already: he finishes there far better than the league, as he did last season.

By Shawn Nafziger · Data through Nov 15, 2026 (14 games and 275 shots this season; 64 games and 1,080 shots in 2025-26)

The take

In a Nov 14 piece on the San Antonio Spurs' 11-game winning streak, Naveen Ganglani wrote that about 43% of Wembanyama's attempts now come at the rim, up from 36% last season, citing stat.space through 13 games. He also notes more paint touches and a jump in scoring, from 25.0 to 28.8 points a game (ALL-STAR).

A center who took 32.4% of his shots from three last season moving inside would be news. Where a player shoots from is also one of the first things that's readable in a season: shot locations settle in about four games. But a single player's shares still wobble a lot on a few hundred shots, so we checked how big this move is against chance.

Is Wembanyama taking more shots at the rim?

A little, and not yet more than chance. Using the NBA's own zone (the restricted area, within about four feet of the hoop), 386 of his 1,080 shots last season came at the rim: 35.7%, the same as Ganglani's 36%. This season it's 112 of 275: 40.7%, a bit below the 43% stat.space shows. The gap may come from how each source draws the rim area.

The rise is +5.0 pp. On 275 shots, his rim share would wobble about ±3.3 pp by chance alone, so the rise is 1.5 times the wobble. Our bar for "too big to be a fluke" is twice the wobble, about 6.6 pp. It's a real possibility, not yet a finding.

The extra rim shots came out of the short paint shots just outside the restricted area (−2.3 pp) and midrange jumpers (−1.2 pp). His share of threes barely moved (32.4% to 31.0%).

How well does Wembanyama finish at the rim?

Very well, and that part isn't luck. This season he's made 79.5% of his 112 shots at the rim, +13.7 pp better than the league's 65.8%: 3.0 times the normal wobble. Last season he made 75.1% of 386 there, +8.0 pp above the league (3.4 times the wobble).

Is he finishing better than last year? He's +4.3 pp above his own 2025-26 rate, only 1.0 times the wobble, so that's within chance. Overall, an average player taking his shots would make 54.9% (), almost exactly last season's 54.8%. His shots aren't much easier than last year; he's simply an elite finisher who's taking a few more of them close in.

When will we know?

Soon, if the change is real. If Wembanyama keeps taking 40.7% of his shots at the rim, the rise clears twice the wobble once he reaches about 590 shots this season: roughly 16 more games at his current pace. We'll check back then.

Ganglani's direction looks right, but the size doesn't hold up yet: by the NBA's zones Wembanyama has gone from 35.7% of his shots at the rim to 40.7%, not 43%, and that's still within chance after 14 games. What's already clear is the finishing: he makes 79.5% at the rim, far above the league. Every extra shot he takes there is one of the best shots in basketball.

Method

  • Data: NBA.com shot locations via the open-source nba_api client: all of 2025-26 (1,080 shots in 64 games) and 2026-27 through Nov 15, 2026 (275 shots in 14 games). This season's numbers are frozen from the site's Player Profile dashboard for that date.
  • Zones: the NBA's own shot zones. "Rim" is the restricted area; "Paint" is the rest of the paint. Other sites (including stat.space) draw their rim area differently, so their shares differ.
  • Change in share: this season's share minus last season's, with a from both seasons' shot counts (√(p₁(1−p₁)/n₁ + p₀(1−p₀)/n₀), 3.3 pp for the rim). Beyond luck = more than 2 standard errors; at the edge = at least 1.6.
  • Rim FG%: vs the league's rim rate each season, with standard error √(p(1−p)/n); vs himself, with both seasons' errors combined (4.4 pp).
  • When we'll know: the number of shots at which a steady 40.7% vs 35.7% would exceed 2 standard errors, at his current 275 ÷ 14 shots a game.
  • Not checked: paint touches (NBA tracking) and stat.space's numbers.
