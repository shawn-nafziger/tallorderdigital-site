Memphis is good. Not +10 good.

The Grizzlies' 6-2 start is real, but a soft schedule and hot shooting on hard shots are doing a lot of the work. Expect a fade.

By Shawn Nafziger · Data through games of Nov 4, 2026

Memphis is a good team. It isn't a +10 team, and the next few weeks will show it.

The Grizzlies are 6-2 with the league's fourth-best , +10.4 points per 100 possessions. That's just outside the for 8 games, so something real is going on. Last season they finished at −5.8. A 16-point jump is a great story. Three things say most of it won't last.

1. Half the schedule was Utah and Sacramento. Memphis has played both twice. Both teams are beyond-luck bad this season (−10.8 and −13.6). Against those two, Memphis is +16.3. Against everyone else it's +4.6, with a 2-2 record that includes home losses to New Orleans and Indiana this week. By , theirs ranks 27th of 30.

2. They're making hard shots at a rate that rarely holds. By location alone, Memphis takes some of the league's worst shots: its ranks 27th. It gets to the rim far less than average (22.6% of shots vs 27.9%) and lives in the short paint instead. Yet it's shooting 3.9 of better than those shots are worth, third-best in the league and at the edge of the luck range.

Some of that is real. Ty Jerome and Cam Spencer were hot shot makers last season too. But if every Grizzly made shots exactly as well as he did last season, the team's would be about +1.4 pp, not +3.9. That difference is worth roughly 4 points per 100 possessions.

3. Nothing underneath is beyond luck. Not one of the , on offense or defense, is outside the luck range or even at its edge. The defense has been helped by opponents shooting 28.9% on above-the-break threes (league: 34.3%). Our own research shows early opponent 3-point shooting tells you almost nothing about later in the season (Check the Take).

My read: good, not that good. The on the Team Ratings dashboard blends this start with last season and lands at +2.8 (90% range −3.7 to +9.3). That's a playoff-race team in the West, not a contender. Claude's read

I'll take the over on +2.8

An AI's take, from the same data, reviewed by Shawn for accuracy. Logged and graded at game 15 and at season's end.

The column is right that the start is inflated. But the estimate still gives last season's −5.8 real weight, and it can't see two things about this roster.

First, the shot making isn't all luck. Jerome and Spencer made tough shots last season too, on 215 and 556 shots, so some of that +3.9 is a skill that should stick. Second, the team's minutes leader is shooting cold. Rookie Cameron Boozer plays 32 minutes a night and is 6.5 pp of eFG% below what his shots are worth. That's within normal , so it should drift back up. When it does, it offsets some of the fade elsewhere.

So: a fade from +10, yes. A fall to +2.8, I doubt it.
