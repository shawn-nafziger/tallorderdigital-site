Movies didn't get longer. Blockbusters did.

The average American movie runs about 100 minutes, just as it did in 1980. The year's biggest hits are another story: they now run more than half an hour longer.

By Shawn Nafziger

Are movies getting longer?

Most aren't. The average U.S. feature film ran 102 minutes in 2020–24, against 99 in 1980–84, and the film, the one in the middle, barely moved (96 to 98 minutes). That's from 23,684 U.S. films listed in .

The biggest hits did get longer. Among the 10 highest-grossing films of each year, the typical live-action film ran 111 minutes in 1980–84 and 143 in 2020–24, going by the running times on Wikipedia. In the early 1980s a big hit ran 15 minutes longer than an ordinary film. Now the gap is 45.

Ordinary films held steady the whole time: in every five-year stretch from 1980 to 2019, the typical U.S. film ran 93 to 96 minutes.

How many blockbusters run 2½ hours or more?

Far more than they used to. Very long movies were rare among the biggest hits: only 2 of the 1980s' top-10 films ran 2½ hours or more. How many do you think the 2020s have had so far?

Of the 60 top-10 films from 2020 to 2025, how many ran 2½ hours or more?

The 2000s had 20, carried by The Lord of the Rings, Pirates of the Caribbean and Harry Potter. The 2020s already have 18 in 6 years, about 3 a year, the fastest pace in the 460 films since 1980.

Do animated movies keep the average down?

Yes. hits run more than half an hour shorter than live-action ones: a typical 100 minutes against 143 in 2020–24. None made the top 10 in 1980–84; since 2005 they have taken 22% of the spots, and 5 of 2024's top 10. Count them in and 2024's typical top-10 film ran 113 minutes; leave them out and it's 128.

Do longer movies need more editors?

Not in the credits. Back in the 1980s, 40% of top-10 films already credited two or more editors: Out of Africa (1985) lists four, and The Fugitive (1993) six. Today's figure is 42%. The typical blockbuster credits 1.7 editors, against 1.5 in the 1980s.

That doesn't mean the work stayed the same. The are only the top of the editing team. Additional editors, assistants and visual-effects editors, where crews really did grow, don't appear in this count.

How to read these "Blockbuster" means one of the 10 highest-grossing films released that year. "Typical" means the median: half the films run longer, half shorter. One very long film can't drag it up. Running times are for the version shown in cinemas, not extended cuts. "Animated" means every frame is animated. Films with real actors on screen, like Space Jam, count as live action.

Notes and sources

  • Data through 2025, read from Wikipedia and Wikidata on Nov 8, 2026.
  • Which films: the top-10 table on each "year in film" page of English Wikipedia (1980–2025), which cites Box Office Mojo. We use the ranking only to choose the films and don't show earnings.
  • Those tables rank by U.S. box office up to 1987 and by worldwide box office from 1988. Since about 2019 the worldwide list includes Chinese hits such as Ne Zha 2.
  • Running time, director and editors: each film's Wikipedia article (the "Edited by" credit). Four hand corrections are listed with reasons in the data repo.
  • All U.S. films: the 23,684 feature films in made in the United States between 1980 and 2025 with a running time of 1 to 4 hours. Wikidata covers well-known films best, so this is "films listed in Wikidata", not every film ever released.
  • Animated or not: Wikidata's "animated film" label, corrected by hand for two films with live actors (Space Jam, Sonic the Hedgehog 3).
