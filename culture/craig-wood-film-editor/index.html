You've seen his work. You probably don't know his name.

Craig Wood has edited pirates, a cartoon lizard, a talking raccoon and the Budweiser frogs. Here are 36 years of his films.

By Shawn Nafziger

Who is Craig Wood?

Craig Wood is an Australian working in Hollywood. From 1989 to 2025 he worked on 28 feature films, 11 of them for one director, Gore Verbinski, including all three original Pirates of the Caribbean films. Five of his films were among their year's 10 biggest at the box office, and two were the year's #1.

Born in Sydney, Wood started at 19 as an assistant editor in the documentary department of Australia's public broadcaster, the ABC, then moved into music videos and commercials, according to his Wikipedia page. In the '90s he cut music videos for Björk, Tom Petty, Janet Jackson and the Smashing Pumpkins.

Which of these did he edit?

Six of these nine are Wood's work. Three were edited by someone else. Pick the three you think aren't his.

The answer: Titanic, Jurassic Park and Frozen were edited by other people. Everything else was Wood: a beer ad with three croaking frogs, a '90s music video, a horror film, a pirate film, a cartoon lizard and a talking raccoon.

How long has he been editing films?

36 years. His first feature, Spirits of the Air, Gremlins of the Clouds, came out in 1989; his newest, Good Luck, Have Fun, Don't Die, in 2025. In between, through the mid-'90s, he cut 13 music videos and a run of TV commercials.

Who has he worked with most?

Gore Verbinski, by far: 11 of his 28 films, from Mouse Hunt in 1997 to Good Luck, Have Fun, Don't Die in 2025, nearly 28 years. No other director has worked with him more than twice. Wood was the only credited editor on six of the 11, shared the credit on four (the three Pirates films and The Lone Ranger) and was an additional editor on one.

Does it matter who's directing?

Yes. On Gore Verbinski's films, Wood was the only credited editor on 6 of 11, more than half. On other directors' films it was 4 of 17: there he usually shared the credit (9 films) or came in as an (4). Of the 10 films he cut alone, 6 were Verbinski's.

Does he cut films alone?

Less and less. Through 2013 he was the only credited editor on 9 of his 17 films. Since 2014 he has 9 of 11, working alongside editors such as Fred Raskin (both Guardians of the Galaxy films), Walter Murch (Tomorrowland) and Dylan Tichenor (Eternals). His most frequent partner in the cutting room is Stephen Rivkin, on all three Pirates films.

That's the change our look at 46 years of blockbusters couldn't find in the credits overall. His films also run long even by blockbuster standards: Pirates of the Caribbean: At World's End, at 168 minutes, is the 6th-longest of the 2000s' 100 top-10 films.

What does a film editor do?

A film editor builds the movie out of the footage that was shot: choosing each take, deciding where every shot starts and ends, and setting the pace of every scene, working closely with the director. Big films often have more than one. On Wood's filmography that shows up as three kinds of credit: editor (the only credited editor), co-editor (sharing the credit with one or two others) and additional editor (brought in to help, credited further down).

American Cinema Editors, an honorary society of film editors, gives the awards each year. Wood has won two: for Pirates of the Caribbean: The Curse of the Black Pearl and for Rango, which also won an Annie Award for its editing.
